Principal Investigator

Dr. Lekhnath Sharma Pathak

Assistant Professor · Central Department of Linguistics, Tribhuvan University

PhD Cognitive Science · University of Hyderabad

Psycholinguistics · Cognitive Science · Pedagogy · Linguistics

Dr. Pathak founded Nepal's first Cognitive Science & Psycholinguistics Lab. His PhD from the Center for Neural & Cognitive Sciences at the University of Hyderabad (under Ramesh Kumar Mishra) focused on bilingual language processing. His research spans child language acquisition, multilingual cognition, dyslexia, psycholinguistic approaches to honorification, and cross-linguistic spatial semantics.

Beyond the lab, Dr. Pathak has been instrumental in advocating for cognitive science as an academic discipline in Nepal. In February 2020, he delivered the keynote “Situation of Illiterates in Nepal: What Does It Mean for Cognitive Science?” at the Third International Workshop of the Society for Cognitive Science of Culture at BITS Pilani, Goa.

Researchers

Mohan Gurung

MPhil-PhD Scholar
Central Dept. of Linguistics, TU

Mohan Gurung works on child language acquisition, with a particular focus on the Gurung language. His doctoral research investigates how Gurung-speaking children acquire language, contributing to a collaborative research project on child language development in an understudied Nepali language.

Sambit Rimal

Graduate Student Researcher
Central Dept. of Linguistics, TU

Website ↗

Sambit Rimal is an MA student in Linguistics at Tribhuvan University with an undergraduate degree in Computer Science and Information Technology. He studies multilingual language processing and cognitive control using process-tracing methods such as mouse-tracking and remote eye-tracking. He is currently working on a Nepali study of agent preference in event cognition and a trilingual study of cognitive control. He was a 2025 CogSci × Gorilla Grant recipient.

Sabita Rijal

Research Assistant
Central Dept. of Linguistics

Sabita Rijal has worked on bilingualism, language of instruction, and cognitive control. Her research has examined how first- and second-language-mediated instruction relates to linguistic performance and executive control, using measures such as verbal fluency, Stroop and Flanker tasks, and mouse-tracking methods.
